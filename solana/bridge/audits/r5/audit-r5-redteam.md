## Findings (the AR series)

### AR1 — medium — a refund lands before kind 6 can, and one stranger's delivery of the refunded send breaks the identity for good

Plan: §5.3 "refund a send by message id (7 days; … a successor's duty for the sends it imported, which the enrolled router no longer delivers, and never a live route's)"; §6.1 "a hold 'stands' for every rule — the deferral of the 7-day group … — from `holdSince` until it ends, never by the live count, while deliveries follow the flags themselves".

Code: `DraftRefund` and `apply` test only `Imported && Refunded == 0` and the lock (hlroute.gno:1195–1213, 1618–1645); the realm cannot see Solana, so the description says "(after kind 6 enrolled this realm)" and nothing else orders it. hlwatch checks a migrate proposal (`migrateCheck`, main.go:271–305) but no refund proposal; its REFUNDED line stands after the fact (views_sol.go:445–448). Nothing in `hlmailbox.Reemit` (mailbox.gno:420–432) or the light-client path refuses a refunded send; only the operator's relayer reads the mark. A send Solana delivered after its refund — or before kind 6, by the relayer, while the predecessor was still the router — leaves supply above the lock by its amount, and no builder moves `lost` but a void (hlroute.gno:703–737).

Scenario: §1's hold: two guardians hold both objects for weeks; GovDAO migrates hlgnot (day 7), proposes kind 6 and the refund of the 40 imported sends (≈300,000 GNOT) together; the refund lands day 14, kind 6 sits Deferred. Day 30 the holders lift: deliveries reopen at the clear, the hold binds 24 hours more (§6.1), kind 6 cannot land. In that day a sender who was refunded calls `Reemit` and delivers his own 25,000 send with the published tool (≈0.03 GNOT, three Solana transactions): minted twice. The monitors read BROKEN by 25,000 for good; the signers stop (§5.6); every exit is by vote for the deployment's life.

Fix: r/hlgov's kind-6 dispatch records in hlmailbox the route it enrolled and the height; the refund builder and `apply` refuse while no such record for this route is 7 days old (necessary, not sufficient — hlwatch alarms a refund proposal whose successor the token has not enrolled, and prints DELIVERED beside any id Solana processed); a `write off` builder (7 days) books a send delivered after its refund as lost, so the identity closes.

### AR2 — medium — before page 0 nothing has moved, yet both doors are one-way: a successor migrated on, or one that never pulls, strands the lock for good

Plan: §5.3 "migrate (the lock, every claim, the sweep entries and the ids of undelivered sends go to a successor realm in pages of 500 records the successor pulls, the lock and the counters with the first page … a range void or a second migrate is refused while pages still arrive … page 0 waiting under a `payouts` flag)".

Code: `DraftMigrate` refuses only while `importing` (hlroute.gno:1221–1229, `mustNotBeImporting` 400–404), which is false until page 0 lands (`Import` 1849–1855); `Decide` queues it with no re-check (1452) and `apply` re-checks only the announcement (1646–1649). `AddRoute` refuses a second migration of a route (flags.gno:326–328); every predecessor builder is `mustNotBeMigrated` (391–395); `Pull` is the named successor's alone (hlgnot.gno:467–484) and pays the lock inside `Export`, so a successor whose `Import` panics reverts the page (migrated: 1850). Nothing re-points a migrate whose successor pulled nothing (`exported == 0`).

Scenario: Day 7 hlgnot's migrate to S applies while an outsider's `payouts` flag (the forgery that prompted it) holds page 0 (R27). S's deployer, a community member, finds its driver refuses every caller but him and goes quiet; GovDAO, reading "a second migrate is refused while pages still arrive", proposes S → S2 on day 8 (S imports nothing, so nothing refuses it); day 15 it applies. S is now migrated: its `Import` panics on every page, so hlgnot's `Pull` reverts for ever; hlgnot's `MigratedTo` is S and cannot change. The whole GNOT lock sits in hlgnot, which refuses every function, with no vote that reaches it. The same end without the second migrate: a successor that never pulls.

Fix: a route registered as a successor (`hlmailbox.Predecessor(self) != ""`) refuses a migrate — at creation, at `Decide` and at `apply` (pending, not spent) — until its import has ended; a predecessor whose successor has pulled nothing (`exported == 0`) accepts a second migrate (7 days) that re-points it, `AddRoute` replacing the record and the copied flags; §5.3 says both.

### AR3 — low-medium — a burn addressed to the successor between kind 6 and page 0 is swept, and the relayer delivers it

Plan: §5.3 "burns made during the migration are addressed to the predecessor until kind 6 enrolls the successor (a week at least), so hlmailbox lets the successor take a message addressed to its predecessor once its import has ended — the message waits undelivered meanwhile"; §5.4 "a credited amount above the lock goes to the sweep queue".

Code: the wait is the successor's `MustHaveImported`, asked only of a message addressed to another realm (hlgnot.gno:235–239; hlroute.gno:1752–1756); a message addressed to the successor itself goes to `Credit`, where before page 0 `importing` is false and the lock 0, so `amount > s.Lock` sweeps it (hlroute.gno:506–524). The relayer's successor-addressed branch waits only while `Importing()` (relayer.go:523–528), never before the first page.

Scenario: Day 7 hlgnot's migrate to S applies under an outsider's `payouts` flag naming a forged flood; page 0 waits (R27) while GovDAO's void as withheld, then as forged, takes two votes — three weeks. Day 7 GovDAO proposes kind 6 (S is registered now); day 14 it lands, and from then every burn names S. The relayer delivers each the hour it is final; S credits nothing — it sweeps: 50 burns a day, each owed and paying nobody until a sweep payment by vote, 256 ids and 7 days a proposal. Day 28 the flag clears and page 0 moves; 700 holders' exits are GovDAO's chore.

Fix: a route registered as a successor refuses every credit until its import has ended — `MustHaveImported` asked of every message, not only another realm's — and the relayer waits on `!mig.Ended()` for a successor-addressed burn as it does for a predecessor-addressed one; §5.3 says a successor credits nothing before its last page.

### AR4 — low-medium — a launch lock under 250,000 compiles a day cap below the per-message maximum, and every send above the cap strands from launch day

Plan: §5.4 "The pausable ISM's own per-message maximum (§13's kind 12) is the same 25,000 at launch and must never be below gno.land's … The day cap is an absolute number while the lock is any size, so at launch it is the smaller of 100,000 and a tenth of the lock the launch notice expects in its first week — the pool's seed"; §11.11 asks Jae for that lock.

Code: `launchDayCap` compiles DEFAULT_DAY_CAP as min(100,000, expected/10) and rewrites nothing else (setup.go:70–78, 159–167); DEFAULT_MAX_PER_MESSAGE stays 25,000 (pausable config.rs:24) and the realms' `launchMaximum` 25,000 (hlgnot.gno:60). `charge` refuses any amount whose level would pass the day cap (state.rs:45–57), so a message above the cap is OverDayCap for ever; the relayer re-marks it OverMax and waits for a raise (relayer_sol.go:348–364). r/hlgov's kind-12 builder refuses a bucket below the maximum (§5.4), a rule the compiled launch values do not obey.

Scenario: Jae answers §11.11 with 150,000 GNOT — a pool seed of ≈$10,000 — so the cap is 15,000 a day for both tokens. Launch day, hour 2: a holder sends 20,000 GNOT; gno.land takes it (the maximum is 25,000). The pausable ISM can never fit it; the relayer parks it "until a kind 12 raise"; a live route's send is never refunded (AQ2). GovDAO's only cures are kind 12 to a bucket above the maximum (a sixth of the lock, against §5.4's own tenth) or a lowering of both maxima (at once on gno.land, 7 days on Solana) that still leaves this send parked for a week. Every send between 15,000 and 25,000 in that week does the same.

Fix: `hlsol setup` compiles DEFAULT_MAX_PER_MESSAGE as min(25,000, the day cap) and refuses an expected lock whose tenth is below the realms' compiled maximum unless `launchMaximum` is set to the same figure; §5.4 states the launch maximum as min(25,000, the day cap) and the notice prints both.

### AR5 — low — the cap-ratio alarm is quiet for as long as the pool never fills, which is exactly when the cap exceeds the lock

Plan: §5.4 "the monitors and `hlwatch` alarm `mint:cap-ratio` when a token's day cap exceeds a fifth of its lock … a forged day's mint before the flags is then bounded by a tenth of the lock"; AUDIT.md round 4 "`mint:cap-ratio` is quiet while a token's lock is still growing toward the first-week expectation it was set against".

Code: `capRatio` answers a note, not an alarm, until `lock >= expected` has been seen once (`Reached`, latched in the state; monitor_sol.go:354–362, views_sol.go:565–567); no clock bounds the quiet. `expectedLock` is each machine's config (config.go:83–86) — the operator's own figure on its monitors, the notice's on the outsiders'.

Scenario: The notice expects 1,000,000 GNOT; the pool takes 60,000 in the first month and stays there for a year. The day cap is 100,000 — 1.7 locks a day — and every monitor and both outsiders' hlwatch print "not judged yet" on the limits line for the whole year; GovDAO gets no prompt to lower it by kind 12. §5.4's "bounded by a tenth of the lock" is, for that year, bounded by 170% of it — the launch-week picture C341 was built to hide, made permanent. An operator who writes a larger figure silences its own monitors the same way.

Fix: the quiet expires: the cap is judged from the later of the lock reaching the expectation and 14 days after `Launch` (hlmailbox's launch time, which the view reads already); §5.4 says a cap above a fifth of the lock a fortnight after launch is lowered by kind 12.

### AR6 — low — the realm walks sixteen migrations, the tools one: a second migration pauses both tokens' deliveries until every config is edited

Plan: §5.3 "hlmailbox lets the successor take a message addressed to its predecessor once its import has ended"; §5.9 "the watch and `hlwatch` read over the pair's summed counters, invariant at every height, never flagging a migrated or importing route alone"; flags.gno:67–69 and 377–389 walk `maxChain` = 16.

Code: `migrationOf` and the relayer's `migrationFor` read the configured route's `migratedTo` and the one realm it names (migrate.go:82–91; relayer.go:483–496) — never that realm's own `migratedTo`. `identityOf` sums that pair (147–158); `judge` flags `mig.Live()` (monitor.go:577–588); `deliveryTarget` delivers to `mig.Successor` (relayer.go:523–534). Nothing follows the route line as `followSuccessors` follows the admin program's (successor_sol.go:43–72).

Scenario: 2028: hlgnot migrated to S in 2027 (the configs still name hlgnot — the pair logic never asked for an edit); S migrates to S2; page 0 moves. That poll both monitors sum hlgnot (0) and S (0) against the live supply: BROKEN — credits and payouts flagged on S, which refuses everything anyway, and the watch keys' flag on the pausable ISM, re-asserted every minute: gno → Solana deliveries of both tokens stop. The relayer delivers every burn to S ("migrated to S2", a deterministic refusal, parked). Both outsiders' hlwatch show BROKEN. Everything stands until the operator and both outsiders edit bridge.json — AQ6's lesson, one layer down.

Fix: `migrationOf`/`migrationFor` follow `migratedTo` to the chain's end (sixteen hops, as the realm), summing every realm of the line and delivering to the last; §5.9 says the tools follow a route's line from the configured path, as they follow the admin program's.

### AR7 — low — the outsiders' only Solana eyes are provider accounts the operator pays "in kind"

Plan: §11.12 "with the operator paying each outsider's two Solana provider accounts in kind (≈$80 a month for both outsiders, counted in §10's band)"; §5.8 "`hlwatch` … alarm its holder directly — from a node the holder runs and two agreeing Solana providers; on anything less it alarms only, and its `payouts` flag command refuses".

Scenario: Launch day: the operator hands each outsider two provider URLs from accounts in its own name. Month 9, §1's leak stand-off: the operator is the party under suspicion (its owner's validator is one of the six). The accounts are the operator's to rotate, throttle or let lapse; an outsider whose two providers stop agreeing, or answer nothing, has an hlwatch that "alarms only" and a flag command that refuses — the brake §3.3 calls the one that counts, switched off by an unpaid invoice. Nothing false is said; the dependence is simply not stated where §8.7 lists what no member can check.

Fix: the outsiders own their provider accounts (and their node) in their own names and the operator reimburses them from the stipend; the launch notice and §8.7 say so, and §11.12's "in kind" becomes "reimbursed".

## Checked and held

- A message credited twice across the pair: the mark is the mailbox's shared `done` (mailbox.gno:458–471), a hand credit on the successor for an id still in a page to come is "done already" and stays pending (hlroute.gno:1705–1707), `Import` refuses a duplicate id (1864–1866); a predecessor-addressed message credited at a successor is one credit at any distance.
- Who registers a successor: `AddRoute` only from a route realm, at a migrate's apply (flags.gno:317–333, `mustBeRoute` 197–206), a route a successor once, never a path that is a route; a chain never cycles; `AnnounceSuccessor` once a path, one symbol, no sub-identity (399–418) — a successor for both routes is refused by the second call.
- The flags following the lock: copied at apply with their ids and ranges (339–354), `Flagged` finds them on the successor; a copy left on the predecessor is cleared by its seat, by GovDAO's mailbox unpause (gov.gno:440–501, which takes any registered route) or with the seat (clearSeat 459–477), so page 0 never waits on a flag no vote can reach.
- The refund floor: summed against the lock at creation and again at apply, pending until the lock covers it (hlroute.gno:1209–1211, 1631–1633); `refund` moves sentTotal and the lock together (1659–1666).
- The cancel's dropped pairs: a number inside a stored range is dead at `execute` (processor.rs:650–658, closed with its buffer returned) and `close_cancelled` is permissionless under the authority PDA (598–622); the executor sends `Execute` for a cancelled number at once (executor_sol.go:35–36), so the rent returns within a tick, nobody's to keep.
- The admin program's line: a forged chain needs an executed kind 9; a loop, a self-successor or a fifth hop is an error (successor_sol.go:57–67), on which the monitor skips its decision rules for the poll (monitor_sol.go:87–89) and the relayer keeps the configured program (relayer_sol.go:209–211) — a kind 9 that executed already holds every authority, so nothing new is lost to it.
- `-record-from client`: the record starts at the client's count, hash, ring and set hash (record.go:262–274) and says so in a standing alarm; hlwatch's client line compares the trusted header with the holder's own node (views_sol.go:537–539, main.go:673), the monitor's record rule does not reach below the checkpoint — the identity and the foreign-decision rules catch what a forged header there carries.
- Version-1 reads (G28): one pin for the scans and the record; a provider's "not supported by the requesting client" is BUILD GAP, never a transient.
- The kind 9 to a successor that cannot inherit (S-18): the successor's state is read at its place before any CPI, spent on four shapes, an error on a wrong account; a retired program opens no stage (S-19) and hlrecover refuses it; the ring keeps its capacity through a stranger's growth (S-17); the body bound 512 at creation and dispatch (hlgov.gno:64, 244–247) against a 538-byte delivery.
- The claims walk resumes and wraps (G34, by its test); `record:scan` and `monitor:no-verdict` alarm after two minutes (G29, G30); the former recipients are refused as successor and recipient at creation and in the closure (hlgov.gno:184–200).
- The economics: 9.0 + 0.15 + 1.5 = 10.65 against 7.36 + 8; the eight sum to the lamport; a successor admin program's 2.03 and a redeployment's ≈10.6 are within the operator's three-month reserve (§11.12) and still unbudgeted as a line.
- The launch sequence: the gno paths are address-namespaced and compiled into the programs before the realms compile the ids; `finish_setup` records the launch set; `Launch` lapses in 14 days; a validator change before `Launch` fires a harmless quarantine. The assent: 241 of 360 is five validators, so one abstention is tolerated and any two unsignable backends kill the recovery — §11.10 says "three of the four must sign", which is that fact.

## Verdict

Honest for a GovDAO member on every ending §1 promises, with two qualifications about the migration, §1's cure for a wedged route, which the code leaves to the order of GovDAO's votes: a refund that lands before kind 6 can (a hold's lift gives a day in which it cannot) lets one stranger mint a refunded send and break the identity for good (AR1); and before page 0 nothing has moved yet neither realm can be re-pointed, so a successor migrated on, or one that never pulls, strands the lock for good (AR2). The rest are small guards: burns swept before a successor's first page (AR3), a small launch lock compiling a cap below the maximum (AR4), the cap-ratio quiet without a clock (AR5), the tools walking one migration (AR6), the outsiders' providers in the operator's name (AR7). All buildable without inventing a rule. Nothing above medium; AQ1–AQ6 hold.
