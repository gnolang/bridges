## 4. Findings (the rules implemented differently or not at all)

**K34 — medium — §5.7 "`ours` requires sender == the enrolled gno router" and §5.3 (v32) "the Go following the successor".** Code: the relayer's `classify` (agent/relayer_sol.go:147-155) accepts a route message only when its sender is the address of the *configured* realm (`rt.Gno`); the enrolled router is never read from the token program and the migration is not consulted on this side. After a migration, from the kind 6 that enrolls the successor as the token's router, every `TransferRemote` through the successor realm dispatches with the successor as sender: the relayer classifies it `kindOther`, writes `Done[nk] = "other"` and advances `GnoNext` past it (relayer_sol.go:234-240; `pruneDone` relayer.go:300-307 then forgets it), and the Reemit rule skips it (relayer.go:863-865). The send stays locked on gno.land with nothing minted; it is not refundable (a refund is for imported sends only, row 43); the monitor counts it in flight by token (chain.go:881) and alarms `age`/`stall` only. A config edit naming the successor as `routes[].gno` delivers later sends but not the nonces already walked past (the relayer's state must be reset), and it then drops any burn still addressed to the predecessor (`deliveryTarget`, relayer.go:518-522). Smallest fix: `classify` accepts a sender that is the configured realm or any successor in its chain (`migrationFor`, read once a tick, as `deliveryTarget` does), and never marks a message `other` while its sender is a route realm; or it reads the token program's enrolled router for gno.land's domain (`sol.ParseToken`, `RouterFor`) and takes that address as `ours`. `TestRelayerFollowsTheMigration` covers the way in only; a test of a send from the successor is the proof.

**K35 — low — §5.3 (v32) "the pause of both, which the watch and `hlwatch` read over the pair's summed counters … never flagging a migrated or importing route alone" against §5.6's refusal rule.** Code: the signers judge the identity over the configured route alone (agent/signer.go:308-318): from page 0 the predecessor's counters are zero against the live supply (or the successor's before page 0, if the configs were edited early), `SignerStops` is true and all three signers halt with a flag-level `signer:halt` ("the supply identity is broken") until their configs name the successor and a verdict holds at or above T. The plan names only the watch and `hlwatch` for the pair reading and says nothing of the signers; the halt costs nothing while the import runs (nothing credits), but it is a false break and a config edit stands between the import's end and the first credit. Smallest fix: the signer reads the pair as the monitor does (`migrationsOf`, `identityOf`) — or the plan states that the operator re-points the three signers at page 0 and expects the halt meanwhile.

**K36 — low — §5.8 (v32) "`hlrecover` in M3d, its reproducible build hash pinned in the launch notice beside `hlwatch`'s, since five validators sign with it".** Code: `hlwatch version` prints the binary's sha256 with its vcs revision and modified flag, at the head of every status (cmd/hlwatch/main.go:143-163, :535); `hlrecover` has no `version` command and prints no hash or vcs stamp (cmd/hlrecover/main.go:65, :75-360) — a validator compares `sha256sum` by hand with no provenance line. Smallest fix: the same `version` subcommand in hlrecover, its line printed by `status` and before `sign`.

**K37 — low — §5.4 (v32) "the monitors and `hlwatch` alarm `mint:cap-ratio` when a token's day cap exceeds a fifth of its lock".** Code: the monitors post `mint:cap-ratio:<token>` (agent/monitor_sol.go:333); `hlwatch` posts `cap-ratio:<token>` (cmd/hlwatch/main.go:1051), so a consumer keyed on the plan's name misses the holder's alarm. Smallest fix: rename hlwatch's key to `mint:cap-ratio:<token>`.
