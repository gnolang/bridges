## Findings (the AT series)

### AT1 — medium-low — the no-hold leak ending's bound is false: a forged kind 12, 8 or 9 lands the same day the forged kind 5 clears the flags, so from day 7 the take is the supply, not a day's cap

Plan: §1 "Without a hold — one awake guardian, or none — a forged replacement of the guardians lands on its own 7th day and clears the flags, and the thief's take is bounded by the day cap per token (§5.4): what minted before the flags, and a day's cap for each day after the lift until the deployment is abandoned"; AUDIT.md's design-change block, what Jae was told: "without a hold a forged replacement of the guardians lands on day 7 with the forger's take bounded by the day caps per token".

Code: the pausable ISM's `set_mint_limits` takes any `max` and `day_cap` from its owner (pausable-ism/src/processor.rs:180–190) and the admin program's kind 12 passes the decision's values through unchecked (admin-program/src/processor.rs:942–953; r/hlgov's own comment: "the admin program and the pausable ISM take any values", hlgov.gno:709–710); kinds 3–9 and 12 are one 7-day group (kinds.rs:56–58), deferred only while a flag or hold stands (processor.rs:689–692); kind 5 lands through a lone flag and clears the replaced keys' flags (763–779, §6.5); a kind 8 from a buffer the forger staged himself (544–578, 802–843) and a kind 9 to a program of his own (844–939) are in the same group.

Scenario: day 0 the holder of the leaked set delivers, under forged headers, two kind 5s naming all four seats and both watch slots, a kind 12 per token at u64::MAX, a kind 8 of the pausable ISM from a no-op buffer and a kind 9 to his own immutable program; the one awake guardian flags both objects (a lone flag, no hold). Day 7 the kind 5s land through the flag and clear it; the kind 12s, the kind 8 and the kind 9 execute in the next transactions (their 7 days passed, no flag stands). From day 7 the side is the thief's exactly as under a lapsed hold on day 21 — the day cap bounded the week, nothing after.

Fix: §1, §9's leak row and the design-change block say the day caps bound the first week only and that with no hold the Solana side is the thief's on day 7; the guardians' standing order then reads "two flags on day 0 or the side is lost in a week". A program brake is possible but is not a bound (the pausable ISM refusing a cap raise above N× the current one and the admin program applying one kind 12 per token per week buys weeks, not safety); the words first.

### AT2 — medium — the refund's Solana gate lives in the relayer's loop, not the realm: anyone's `Execute(id)` applies a matured refund on the note alone, and a kind 6 deferred by one flag makes the double mint

Plan: §5.3 "the gate is Solana's: `hlwatch` reads the token's enrolled router … and the relayer executes no refund while the token's router is not the refunding successor (a tick's wait, never a park)" — beside §5.3's own "anyone applies them by calling `Execute(id)` after the delay (the relayers do)".

Code: `Execute` is anyone's and gates a refund on a `payouts` flag alone (hlgnot.gno:493–501, hlgnot2.gno:491–499); `apply(refundParams)` asks `mustBeEnrolled` — the note's 14 days — and pays (hlroute.gno:1586–1597, 1761–1786, 1251–1259); the router read and the kind 6's state are `refundGate`, called inside the relayer's own `executes` (relayer.go:840–848, 875–910) and hlwatch's alarm (hlwatch/main.go:523–545); enrol.go:14–21 says why the note is not enough. The realm cannot see Solana and no realm rule reads the relayer's verdict.

Scenario: hlgnot → hlgnot2, the pages in. Day 0 the kind 6 for hlgnot2 is dispatched (the note written, flags.gno:505–517) and delivered. Day 1 a watch key strokes `header:mismatch` on a transient provider disagreement — the stroke flags both objects (monitor.go:853) — or a guardian flags the admin program by judgment: the kind 6 is deferred (processor.rs:689–692) until an unpause GovDAO votes and that lands 24 hours after delivery. Day 14 a member proposes the refund of the 40 imported sends; the note is 14 days old and the realm takes it; hlwatch alarms NOT ENROLLED ON SOLANA, "cancel it while this stands", every poll; GovDAO, two votes away, has not cancelled by day 21. Day 21 a refunded sender — his id is in the proposal's description — calls `Execute(id)` himself: the realm pays him back and `sentTotal` and the lock fall (hlroute.gno:1784–1785, 1823–1830). He then delivers his original message on Solana with the published tool (a `Reemit`, a header proof; the relayer refuses a refunded send, a stranger's delivery passes): hlgnot is still the enrolled router, the pausable ISM meters it, the token mints. The lock is short by his amount for good; the write-off books it lost (1791–1807). Per id he holds, bounded per delivery by the per-message maximum and the day cap.

Fix: the realm's apply of a refund is GovDAO's at-once act, not anyone's `Execute`: a `NewApplyRefundRequest(id)` builder in both routes and hlgnot2 (at once; the closure runs `st.Execute` for a `KindRefund` decision past its delay; `Execute(id)` refuses the kind), the member reading hlwatch's gate before the vote — where the plan already puts the member; §5.3 then says the realm's gate is the note and GovDAO's apply, never the relayer's loop. Until it ships, the standing order: a guardian flags `payouts` on the refunding realm while hlwatch shows NOT ENROLLED ON SOLANA (the one gate `Execute` honours, hlgnot.gno:496–497), and hlwatch's alarm says to.

### AT3 — medium — the fresh deployment both endings rest on cannot receive r/hlgov's decisions: a new admin program's sequence starts at 0, r/hlgov's counter never goes down, and a new r/hlgov can never write the mailbox's enrolment note

Plan: §6.4 "GovDAO's exit is a fresh deployment: new programs and new synthetic tokens … the realms stay, pointed at the new deployment by the routes' `set router` and r/hlgov's `set decision recipient` (7 days each)"; §1's leak ending "a rotation of the set on gno.land and a fresh deployment"; §5.3 "a `sync sequence` builder … never lowers it … a fresh r/hlgov for a new chain is deployed with its predecessor's last number compiled in" (a new chain, not a new deployment on this one).

Code: `init` writes `last_seq: 0` (admin-program/src/processor.rs:186) and `Init` carries no number (instruction.rs; sol/admin.go:83 `InitIx(payer, authority, seats, watch, origin, predecessor)`); the only writer of another starting number is `successor_init`, signed by the predecessor's authority PDA alone (203–223), reached by kind 9's CPI through the client (844–939) — no proof, no number; `handle` refuses a number above last+1 (`OutOfOrder`, 398–400) and ignores one at or below (388–391); r/hlgov's `dispatch` takes `last+1` (hlgov.gno:255–264) and `mustRaise` refuses a lower number (738–748); `NoteEnrolled` and `ClearEnrolled` accept r/hlgov's compiled path alone (flags.gno:487–495; mailbox.gno:57), and hlmailbox is immutable.

Scenario: the client lapses (or the set leaks) after r/hlgov's 37th decision. GovDAO follows §6.4: six new programs, the setup step, the audit (which prints "r/hlgov's counter 37 beside the admin program's 0 with the gap", §5.8 — a line, no refusal), `set router` on each route and `set decision recipient` to the new admin program, 7 days. The first decision after — a kind 12, a kind 5, the kind 6 that would enrol a successor so the stranded sends can be refunded — goes out as #38; the new program expects #1 and answers `OutOfOrder`; the message stays deliverable for ever and every decision after it with it. `sync sequence` cannot lower the counter. A new r/hlgov at a new path reaches the new admin program only if that program's `HLGOV_SENDER` was compiled to it — a third deployment (≈8.5 SOL, the launch procedure again) — and that r/hlgov can never write the enrolment note, so no refund of an imported send is possible on this chain again (the realm's gate is the note, hlroute.gno:1251–1259). The exit costs two launches and loses the refund path.

Fix: `Init` takes a starting number — `Init { …, last_seq u64 }` written at processor.rs:186 — zero for the first deployment, r/hlgov's `Last()` for a fresh one under the same r/hlgov, set and printed by `hlsol setup`, printed by `hlsol audit` (its gap line a refusal at a launch where the two differ) and read by §8.3 as "its sequence number matches r/hlgov's counter"; §6.4 says a fresh deployment on the same chain keeps r/hlgov, starts its admin program at r/hlgov's number, and dispatches nothing to the old recipient between the deploy and the switch (a number taken in between is cured upward by `sync sequence`, as today).

### AT4 — low — §6.4 says nothing of the sends locked after the client's last proven header: unrefundable on the live route, refundable only by a migrate nobody has written down

Plan: §6.4 "then gno → Solana stops for good … Burns back to gno.land keep working … what the lock cannot cover is a credit by vote"; §5.3 a refund is "a successor's duty for the sends it imported … never a live route's: a stranded send there waits for a kind 12".

What works: a migrate (7 days), anyone's `Adopt` of the pages, a kind 6 dispatched for the successor (its note lands though the decision never does), 14 days, a refund (7 days) applied by anyone's `Execute` — five weeks, nowhere written, and only because the realm's gate is weaker than §5.3 says.

### AT5 — low — a diverted credit before a checkpoint is caught by the other monitor alone: hlwatch compares a claim with its dispatch record only for a void

The brief's question — a forged claim credited at or below the checkpoint's height on a fresh install: what catches it. Plan: §5.9 "a credited claim whose recipient or amount differs from its Solana dispatch record" flags (the monitors'); §5.8 hlwatch indexes "Solana's dispatched-message accounts by message id" and shows "every void as forged of a claim whose Solana dispatch record matches it".

Code: `divertedCredits` compares the ids the index holds (monitor.go:1247–1283); `judgeUnindexed` trusts outright a claim at or below `IndexFromHeight` when the mailbox's accounts could not be listed (checkpoint.go:235–245; the 403 the assumptions audit measured); hlwatch's one recipient-and-amount compare serves `VoidsMatch` (views_sol.go:567–568) and its forged verdict is the unindexed rule alone (hlwatch/main.go:1437–1452; no "diverted" anywhere under cmd/hlwatch).

Scenario: a pure forgery — an id no dispatch carries — breaks the identity on every machine and on hlwatch (the lock short of supply + owed): caught. A diversion — two signers credit a real burn's id to another address — moves no supply; the fresh monitor under the fallback trusts it (before the base), the seeded path or the other monitor catches it; both monitors rebuilt from a checkpoint on providers refusing the listing, nothing automatic does, and the outsiders' hlwatch never did: the victim's complaint is the detector of the one forgery only the operator's signers can make.

Fix: hlwatch's view compares every open verifier-credited claim it indexes with its dispatch record (the compare at views_sol.go:567 lifted into a `Diverted` list, alarmed and offered to `flag -ids`), §5.8 lists it, and the monitors' fallback text says the other machine is the check.

## Verdict

Nothing high. Two mediums at the seams the brief named: the refund's Solana gate is the relayer's loop while the realm's `Execute` is anyone's, so a kind 6 deferred by one flag lets a refunded sender mint his send twice on day 21 (AT2); and the fresh deployment both endings sell cannot be governed, since a new admin program starts its sequence at 0, r/hlgov's counter never goes down, and a new r/hlgov can never write the enrolment note (AT3) — each cured by a small change (an at-once GovDAO apply of a refund; an `Init` that takes the starting number). One medium-low on the ending Jae was told: without a hold the thief's take after day 7 is the supply, not a day's cap, since the forged kind 12, 8 and 9 land with the kind 5 (AT1; the words). The rest are guards and wordings (AT4–AT8). The removal itself is clean: no settled set, window or counter survives on chain, the hand-over carries what a successor needs, a kind 9 is no exit for a lapsed client, and burns back keep working on the signers. AS1–AS8 hold at their lines.
