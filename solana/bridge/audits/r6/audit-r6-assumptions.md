## Findings

### F27 (medium, realm + Go + plan) — the enrolment note's 7 days always expire before kind 6 executes on Solana, and nothing reads Solana to close the gap

**The assumption** (PLAN §5.3:46 "a refund is refused until that record is 7 days old — before it the predecessor's relayer may still deliver"; `realm/p/hlroute/v0/hlroute.gno:1229-1231` "the admin program executes a kind 6 within the week unless a hold defers it, which hlwatch shows"): that 7 days after r/hlgov dispatched the kind 6, the token on Solana enrolls the successor, so a refunded send is no longer deliverable.

**The source that settles it**: `hlroute.gno:1233-1241` (`mustBeEnrolled`: `now < at + decisions.Delay` — 7 days of gno block time from the note's time, `at` = `now()` at the dispatch, `flags.gno:440`); PLAN §6.5:172-177 and `kinds` — kind 6 is of the 7-day group: it "matures 7 days after delivery" by the Clock, "a lone flag defers execution of the 7-day group" (§6.5:184-185), a hold defers it to the hold's end; the delivery is after the dispatch by the updater's header and the relayer's staged proof (minutes to an hour; the executor executes on its tick). `cmd/hlwatch/main.go:262-313` (`refundCheck` reads `mb.Enrolled[realm]` — hlmailbox's note — and the processed ids; no read of the token's enrolled router: `RouterFor` is absent from hlwatch; the relayer's `checkRouters`, `relayer_sol.go:161-190`, alarms only a router that is no realm of the line, and the predecessor is one). `agent/relayer.go:788-810` (`executes` calls `Execute(id)` on every queued decision the moment its delay has passed — the refund applies at the first tick after the note's 7th day, automatically).

**The check**: the two clocks compared. The realm's floor is `at + 7 d` (block time). Kind 6 executes at `delivered + 7 d` (Clock) + any deferral, and `delivered > at` always. So the refund applies before kind 6 executes by at least the delivery latency — in the normal case minutes; under any lone flag on the admin program (a watch key's own stroke on an invariant break lands on both objects) for the flag's whole life; under a hold 21 days or more. In that window the predecessor is still the enrolled router: the refunded send's message is deliverable by anyone's `Reemit` (permissionless) and a delivery with the published tool (the operator's relayer refuses by the mark, `relayer_sol.go`; a stranger's does not; the operator's own updater feeds the header). The refunded sender is the natural attacker: his refund and his mint, 25,000 of the token a send, every send on the refund list (bounded by the lock, R30). hlwatch's "ENROLLED … UNDER THE 7-DAY DELAY" goes quiet on the same clock as the realm, so the member sees nothing wrong, and the write-off (lost += amount) closes the identity by booking the lock short.

**Verdict**: FALSE. The 7 days are a floor, never the guarantee; AR1's window reopens by the latency and reopens whole under a flag.

**What changes if it is false / the smallest fix**: (1) `refundCheck` reads each token's enrolled router for gno.land's domain on Solana (`sol.ParseToken`, `RouterFor`, as `checkRouters` does) and alarms NOT YET ENROLLED ON SOLANA while it is not the successor (the realm's 7 days as a floor beneath it); (2) `executes` does not call `Execute(id)` on a refund decision while the router is not the successor (a tick's wait, never a park; `hlrecover`/hlwatch say why); (3) §5.3's sentence: "7 days after the note, and never before the token enrolls the successor on Solana, which hlwatch and the relayer read" — and the realm comment's "within the week" corrected.

### F28 (medium-low, realm + plan) — "before page 0 nothing has moved" is false for the dropped successor's own sends: a re-point strands them for good

**The assumption** (PLAN §5.3:47 "a predecessor whose successor has pulled nothing accepts a second migrate that re-points it, so no lock is ever stranded on a realm nobody may pull from"; AUDIT.md's round-5 line "before page 0 nothing has moved"; M1-NOTES M1h/AR2): that a successor registered by a migrate holds nothing of its own until it pulls page 0, so dropping its registration loses nothing.

**The source that settles it**: `realm/hlgnot/hlgnot.gno:172-203` (`TransferRemote`: `mustBeLive`, `mustNotBePaused(FnSends)`, `CheckSend` — no gate on the realm's own import; a successor built from this tree's routes takes a send from the block its migrate applied, the coins locked in it, `st.Send` adding to its `SentTotal` and `Lock`); `realm/p/hlroute/v0/hlroute.gno:415-425` (`CheckSend` refuses only a migrated route); `hlmailbox/flags.gno:324-356` (`AddRoute`: the re-point `dropRoute(old)` — "it is a route no more"); `hlroute.gno:1211-1216` (a route's own send is never refunded: "waits for a kind 12"); PLAN §5.3:128 (kind 6 "accepts only a registered route's address"); `AddRoute`'s `mustBeRoute(cur)` (the dropped realm can never migrate on).

**The check**: the gates read. Between the first migrate's apply and the re-point — a week at least, the re-point being a 7-day migrate — the first successor S1 is a registered route whose sends the mailbox dispatches with S1 as sender; Solana never enrolls S1 (kind 6 needs a registered route, and after the drop it is none), so such a send waits for a kind 6 that can never come; S1 cannot refund it (its own send), cannot migrate it (no longer a route), and no builder reaches S1. The user's coins stay in S1's balance for good. The plan's own sentence promises the opposite.

**Verdict**: FALSE for sends through the dropped successor; true for the lock, the counters and the records (page 0 moves them).

**What changes / the smallest fix**: a successor takes no send before its last page is in — `TransferRemote` (both routes) asks `hlmailbox.Predecessor(own path)` and refuses while `imported == 0 || importing`, the mirror of `MustTake` (`hlroute.gno:1897-1905`) — and §5.3 says so beside "a successor credits nothing before its first page". Then nothing of anyone's is ever in a successor a re-point drops.

### F29 (low, Go) — `ringInOrder` rotates by `ring_next` only when the mirror's count reaches `RingCapacity`, which can exceed 256 mid-growth

**The assumption** (`agent/checkpoint.go:33-48`: "a full ring's oldest entry sits at ring_next … so the mirror rotates by it"; the rotation runs when `len(ring) >= capacity`, `capacity = sol.RingCapacity(cs.Head, cs.Len)`): that the account's ring capacity equals the ring the program fills.

**The source that settles it**: `programs/gno-light-client-ism/src/state.rs:152-160` (`Ring::push`: `cap = capacity(head, len).min(RING)` — the program's ring is at most 256 entries whatever the account holds); `processor.rs:183-200` (`grow`: the account is resized by up to 10,240 bytes a call and the body region moves only once "the account holds the new region and a ring of the capacity it had", so between the resize and the move the ring region is the old one plus the whole growth: `Ring::capacity` = 256 + 142 for one step); `sol/client.go:756-762` (`RingCapacity` = `(dataLen − off) / RingEntryLen`, no `min(…, Ring)`).

**The check**: in the steady state the account is `full_len(body_cap)` exactly (`grow` stops at `target`), so `RingCapacity` = 256 and the rotation runs whenever the ring is full. In the window between a growth's resize and its relocation, `RingCapacity` is above 256 while the program's full ring holds 256: `len(ring) >= capacity` is false, the mirror takes slot order, and F21's divergence returns for 256 pushes if the checkpoint is read then — a window of seconds, once per growth (a set-history growth, rare), only with a wrapped ring.

**Verdict**: FALSE in a narrow window; VERIFIED in the steady state.

**The smallest fix**: `capacity = min(capacity, sol.Ring)` in `ringInOrder` (one line; `RingCapacity`'s comment "256 once grown" already means it).
