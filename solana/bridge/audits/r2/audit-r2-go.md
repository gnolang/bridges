## Findings

### G13 — high — a signer signs a burn its verdict never covered: the one-round window AN3 was meant to close is still open
agent/signer.go:282–303 (only burns with `rec.d.Slot <= snap.Slot` count as uncredited; a burn above the snapshot's slot counts as nothing), 476–500 (`signSol` signs every unsigned dispatch both providers show, with no look at the slot the verdict covered), 156–160 (`signing()` checks only the halt and the verdict's age).
Scenario: a forged header has let an attacker mint F on Solana. Each signer round reads the supplies at finalized slot s, waits for a gno block, reads the routes, then scans the dispatches. The attacker sends mint-and-burn-toward-gno.land in one transaction; it finalizes at slot x > s but before the scan. The supply at s is the lock (holds); the burn at x is not uncredited (x > s); `signSol` signs it in the same round. Next round the signer halts — too late, the signature is served. Each signer independently has this window every round (the gno-block wait alone is 0–3.4 s), so a 2-of-3 threshold is met about half the time; the relayer delivers `Process` and the forged credit lands as a claim.
Fix: keep the verdict's snapshot slot (`s.verdictSlot = snap.Slot` under the lock) and in `signSol` skip (return, not drop) a dispatch with `rec.d.Slot > s.verdictSlot`: it is signed one round later, after a cut that counted it. Test: a dispatch above the snapshot's slot is not signed until the next held cut.

### G14 — high — the monitor's record scan runs ahead of its snapshot; a dozen sends in two minutes flag both Solana objects (`header:gap`)
agent/record.go:148–168 (`Sync` folds every transaction finalized at listing time, unbounded by the snapshot's slot), 257–273 (`Consistent`: the scan ahead falls to "N entries rebuilt, the client counts N−1", never "ahead"); agent/monitor_sol.go:59–78 (`Sync` runs after the snapshot; any non-ok, non-behind answer goes to `seenFor("header:gap", 2 min)` → `flagSolana(…, adminToo)`; `m.seen["header:gap"]` is deleted on ok only, never on "behind"); agent/updater.go:189–207 (a header goes up every tick while a dispatch waits, so a steady flow lands a header every ~10 s).
Scenario: twelve sends ten seconds apart (≈60 GNOT in fees) make the updater accept a header every tick. Every poll the monitor's snapshot counts `records` at a finalized slot ~13 s old, then `Sync` folds the header finalized since: `Count = records + 1`, not ok, not behind. No poll is ok for two minutes, `persisted` turns true and the watch key flags the pausable ISM and the admin program: a pause only an unpause decision clears, bought for $4. A fresh monitor is worse: minutes of "behind" leave `m.seen` standing, and its first ahead poll flags at once.
Fix: give `Sync` a `maxSlot` (the snapshot's `cs.Slot`) and stop before the first signature above it, leaving the cursor there (the listing is newest-first; after the reverse, break on `s.Slot > maxSlot`); in `solChecks` delete `m.seen["header:gap"]` on "behind" too. Test: a record scan with a transaction past the snapshot slot is consistent at the snapshot.

### G15 — medium — the Solana-side rules of step 7 read past the cut and flag without persistence: a lagging own node makes a permanent `header:mismatch`, a decision at the wrong moment a false `decision:foreign`
agent/record.go:282–302 (`Check` pops an entry whose height the own node has not reached — `hash == "" && e.Height > head` — and keeps the `Mismatch` "until a human looks"; record_test.go:125 pins it); agent/monitor_sol.go:62–67 (`flagSolana(…, adminToo)` the moment `mm != nil`, no `persisted`, unlike step 2's "ahead" rule at monitor.go:287–303); monitor_sol.go:120–133 (the admin state is a fresh finalized read, r/hlgov is read at the cut's H).
Scenario: the backup monitor's gno node stalls 14 s (inside `headAfter`'s 15 s and far inside `ownLagAfter`'s five minutes); the updater on the other machine accepts header h+3 meanwhile. `Sync` folds it, `Check` asks the stalled node for h+3, gets "past the head", pops the entry and flags the pausable ISM and the admin program at once, with the entry never re-checked and `Mismatch` written to the state file. Likewise a GovDAO decision dispatched after H and delivered before step 7's admin read is "a number r/hlgov never dispatched": flagged on both objects and kept in hlwatch's `Foreign` list for good.
Fix: in `Check`, leave an entry above the head in `unchecked` (return, no mismatch: step 2 judges a client past the chain with the allowance and two minutes); gate the record's flag through `persisted("header:mismatch", cutFailAfter)`; in `foreignFindings` re-read a nil body at the own node's current head before calling a number foreign.

### G16 — medium — the providers' API keys travel in error text to the webhook, the logs and the status endpoints
sol/rpc.go:20 (the provider URL, query string included, goes to solana-go's client, whose errors say `rpc call X() on <URL>: …` — jsonrpc.go:552–619 — and net/http's `url.Error` prints the URL too); agent/signer.go:223,231,342 (`unverified` → `Alarm("signer:no-verdict", why)`); agent/monitor.go:255,261 and monitor_sol.go:247 (`solana:providers`, `flag:solana` with `err.Error()`); agent/relayer.go:318 and relayer_sol.go:332 (`parked:` with `%v`); agent/alarm.go:100–127 (every alarm is a JSON POST to the webhook, "the box's chat hook is one consumer"); signer.go:614, monitor.go:887 (`providers` verbatim on the status); cmd/hlbridge/main.go:46,110,119,139 (the relayer's, monitor's and updater's status listeners are not forced to loopback).
Scenario: a paid provider's URL is `https://…/?api-key=K` (the common form). Its first timeout posts `"the Solana snapshot: BUBBLE supply from provider 2: rpc call getTokenSupply() on https://…/?api-key=K: context deadline exceeded"` to the operator's chat hook, hourly while it lasts, and to every log. Anyone reading that channel, or a status endpoint bound past loopback, holds the key: they exhaust the quota (a signer halts after an hour without a verdict) or read the provider's request logs. §5.6's provider independence rests on those tokens.
Fix: a `redact(text)` that strips query strings and userinfo from every `https?://` in alarm, note, status and log text (one place: `Alarmer.raise` and the `note`/`set` helpers), drop `providers` from the status, and refuse a non-loopback `-listen` for the relayer, monitor and updater as the signer's status already does.

### G17 — medium — one provider's slot blinds a monitor for good: a dispatch's slot is taken at first sight and never compared
agent/monitor.go:636–647 (`indexDispatches`: a record already indexed only gets `rec.Providers |= 1 << i`; `Slot` is whichever provider showed it first), 358–360 (`rec.Credited != 0 && rec.Slot > snap.Slot` → `CreditedAfterSlot`), 373–377 (no verdict); the same code feeds hlwatch (views_sol.go:290).
Scenario: provider 2 shows a real burn a few slots before provider 1 finalizes it (ordinary skew) and lies about its slot (2⁶²). The record is written with that slot and persisted. Once the burn is credited, every poll ends "burns credited at height H were dispatched after slot S": the order never holds, `cut` alarms after two minutes, no identity verdict, no Solana-side rule runs — for ever, since nothing re-reads the slot. A forged credit later goes unflagged by this monitor. No disagreement is reported, because none is looked for. (The signer's provider 1 lying the same way halts it within the hour, as §5.6 accepts.)
Fix: when the second provider shows an indexed record, compare its slot, amount and recipient; on a difference alarm `solana:providers`, mark the record disputed, and leave a disputed record out of the cut and the identity (neither in flight nor credited-after) until both agree; hlwatch prints it UNCONFIRMED.

### G18 — medium — the executor's `ExtendProgram` has no bound, no backoff and no alarm: a refused extend is retried hot every tick, a large one drains the relayer's float silently
agent/executor_sol.go:142–148 (the "extend" branch sends and `continue`s: no `attempt`, no `act`, no count), 206–215 (`ExtendProgramIx(p.Program, executor, ExtendStep)`: the relayer's key pays); sol/loader.go:19 (10,240 bytes ≈ 0.072 SOL of rent a step).
Scenario: GovDAO's kind 8 names a buffer 150 KB longer than the program data. Execute answers NeedsExtend; every tick the primary (and, a minute later, the backup) sends a step: fifteen steps ≈ 1.1 SOL from the 2 SOL float, with no "about to spend" line — the runway alarm fires after. If the loader refuses the step for good (the program data at its bound, a successor already named), the branch retries every 3 s for ever with no parking, no alarm beyond a note, and a simulation per tick against the provider. §5.7's "a deterministic refusal parks after three" does not reach this call.
Fix: give the extend its own action key through `tryCall`-style bookkeeping (`a.fail` with `solDeterministic`, parked after three); compute the steps needed from the buffer's length minus the program data's before the first step and alarm `execute:extend:<decision>` with the SOL it will cost; let the backup extend only after its offset.

### G19 — low — nothing watches gno.land's validator count against the client's real bound (~50); the only alarm fires at a region size a set of 50 never reaches
agent/updater.go:429–433 (`updater:body-bound` only when `BodyCapNeeded(n) >= MaxBodyCap`); sol/client.go:44,561–566 (81,920 bytes; a body of five 50-validator entries wants ≈17 KB); PLAN §6.1 ("about 50 through any history … due before gno.land nears 50 validators"); hlwatch and the monitor print the set size and judge nothing.
Scenario: a GovDAO vote raises the set from 6 toward 50 over months. No machine says a word until the announcing update's `UpdateFinish` fails inside the program's 32 KB heap; the client then cannot follow the chain, the quarantine never starts, deliveries stop and the lapse window opens in 14 days — the failure lands on the operator with no warning, exactly the "limit that fails on someone else" the memory rule names.
Fix: one line in `planUpdate`/`tick` and in hlwatch's client line: alarm `client:set-bound` when the current or announced set has 40 or more validators ("the client's measured bound is about 50; the heap frame is due"), and print the count beside the bound.

### G20 — low — the compiled bindings of the three programs (the pausable ISM's MAILBOX and TOKENS, the client's MAILBOX_PKG_PATH, the admin program's HLGOV_SENDER) are never checked on the deployed bytes; the ELF line compares with the file that was deployed
cmd/hlsol/setup.go:126–176 (`launchValues` writes the ids into config.rs before the build); cmd/hlsol/audit.go:123–132 (`fsum == sum` against `-so DIR`, a note without it); local/e2e.sh:244,681 (the audit's `-so` is the same `$SBF` the setup deployed from: the line cannot fail).
Scenario: a member runs `hlsol audit` as §5.8 asks. Every line is ok, the ELF hashes match the files beside them, and nothing on the chain says which mailbox or tokens the pausable ISM meters or which realm the client believes is hlmailbox: a build from a config.rs with another token id passes the whole audit, and the mint limit of AN5 then meters a token nobody delivers to. The only check is a rebuild by hand from the launch values, which the audit neither does nor demands.
Fix: in the audit, read each deployed ELF (already fetched) and FAIL unless the pausable ISM's bytes contain the mailbox id and both token ids, the client's the mailbox pkgpath, the admin program's the mailbox, the tokens and r/hlgov's 32 bytes (all `-mailbox-pkgpath`/`-keys` already give); in the e2e, run one audit with `-so` pointing elsewhere.

### G21 — low — hygiene: a stage leaks on a late confirmation, plain-http public listeners, an unchecked index error behind a forged verdict, a password on argv, "sequence" as transient, a shutdown that waits on `Send`
agent/relayer_sol.go:524–531 (the stage is closed only after `Send` returns; sol/sol.go:339–352's "not confirmed in a minute" is transient, the next pass finds the message delivered and never closes it: ≈0.01 SOL a time, for ever); cmd/hlbridge/main.go:64–82,92–99 (the signer's public `-listen` serves plain HTTP when `-tls-cert` is absent, on any address; only the client refuses it); cmd/hlwatch/main.go:725–759 (`flag -ids` refuses on `Unconfirmed` but not on `v.Err`, so a dispatch index that failed on one provider still yields "forged: no dispatch record on either provider"); cmd/hlrecover/main.go:88 (`-auth-password` visible in `ps`; the env form exists); gno/gno.go:310 (`transientRE` matches "sequence", so a realm refusal wording a sequence — kind 10's — is retried every minute and never parks); sol/sol.go:339–352 (`Send` sleeps 60 × 500 ms ignoring ctx: SIGTERM waits a minute).
Scenario: a busy day with a few RPC timeouts leaves a dozen proof stages open at 0.01 SOL each; a signer deployed without `-tls-cert` on 0.0.0.0 serves signatures in the clear (§5.6 says HTTPS); a guardian names an id forged on a day one provider is down.
Fix: on a message found delivered, close its stage if the account still stands; refuse a non-loopback signer `-listen` without `-tls-cert`; refuse `flag -ids` while `v.Err` names the dispatch index; drop the password flag for the env var; remove "sequence" from gno's `transientRE` in favour of the exact "invalid sequence" text; make `Send`'s wait a `select` on ctx.

## Checked and held

- Keys: every key file goes through `CheckKeyFile` (0600, non-empty); seeds and keys are never printed or posted (public keys only); `hlkey new` refuses to overwrite; the deployer's key reaches the CLI by path and is left holding nothing but the floats' change; hlrecover's signature file carries pubkey, digest and signature, and a YubiHSM signature is verified locally before it is written.
- The relayer's key alone pays stagings, process, Execute, Grow, ExtendProgram and Settle — all permissionless; it holds no seat and cancels nothing. The watch key is a pause lever and nothing more, as §5.6 says.
- Transport: signer fetches refuse plain http unless loopback or `allowHTTP`, 1 MB and 10 s; gno RPC reads 8 MB and 30 s; every Solana call 30 s; gnokey 90 s; `/v1/sol/<nonce>` parses an integer.
- Provider agreement: supplies and the client's head from both providers; a dispatch is signed only once both scans show the same id (keccak of the bytes, so the same bytes); the pair compares owner and data of accounts and slot, failure, bytes, loaded addresses and inner instructions of transactions; `hlbridge providers` enforces no shared pair and no provider in three.
- The ordered cut: the finalized snapshot at the lower slot, a gno block after it, every render at that height, the burns credited above the snapshot's slot breaking the order; T inside the allowance judged at the head, past it by a second source with two minutes' persistence; the identity never touches `sends`, never a void; `sends` is mirrored only for paused deliveries and cleared after.
- The signers' rules: once per message by the store, a nonce conflict sticky and still served, no signature before a first verdict, the tight rule with uncredited burns, the stop clearing on a held cut; the halt alarm once and hourly.
- The relayer: exactly the rule's signatures, each verified against hlmailbox's key; `Claim(20)` with a doubling backoff; `Execute(id)` by block time; three deterministic failures park with hourly retries and a transient never counts; one copy per number and only the next expected; `HandleAccounts` from the simulation with any signer or the payer refused; the admin program's metas checked; cancels capped at 16.
- The executor: `MaturesAt`/`FloorUnder` are the program's `replacement_floor` (the same arithmetic, 29 cases); NotMature/Deferred wait; CancelSpent/LayoutFixed are never retried; kinds 9, 11 and 12 take `ExtraFor`'s accounts; Settle is computed locally first, `NothingToSettle` is tolerated, both machines may call it.
- The updater: its own key and float; the announcing header found by binary search and verified against NextValidatorsHash; a trusted header that announces is reported; Grow before an announcing update, twelve calls at most; the chunked next set resumed by length and hash; the lag alarm at an hour.
- hlrecover: the digest is the tool's claim and `recovery_record` recomputes it from the staged body (recovery.rs:315–322), so a wrong body is refused on chain; a reset refused while others pend unless `-force`; submit checks each key against the recorded settled set and verifies the signature before sending; `settle` says why not.
- hlsol setup: §5.8's binding order, every step read from the chain first, a second run sends nothing (the e2e asserts it), the burn last, `finish_setup` after it, done only on the audit's PASS; the audit prints every call as curl and compares the trusted hash and the set hash with the node.
- State files: temp file, fsync, rename, directory fsync; a zero-length or unparseable file named as corruption; the e2e kills and restarts the primary on its state file. `go test -race ./...` green. The e2e never exercises a real Settle, kinds 8/9/11's Execute, a hold ending by a dip, a replacement at the floor, a crash mid-stage, a refused chunk, `successor-init` on a chain, a stale stage rebuilt, a multi-chunk leaf or next set, a forged header, the monitor's Solana flag, TLS or the YubiHSM; the programs' suite covers the first four, the Go units `stageMatches`, `executeWhen` and `judgeAhead`, nothing covers the rest.

## Verdict

Nothing critical: the chains still hold the money. Two highs are gaps the round-1 fixes left open — a signer signs a burn its verdict never covered (AN3's one-round window, G13), and a dozen sends in two minutes buy a two-object pause through the monitor's own record scan (G14). Four mediums: the step-7 rules flag without the cut's patience (G15), provider keys reach the webhook (G16), one provider's slot blinds a monitor for good (G17), the extend path has no bound (G18). Fold G13–G18 before devnet; the e2e should add a burst of sends and a lagging own node.
