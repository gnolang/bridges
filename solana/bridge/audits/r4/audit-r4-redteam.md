## Findings (the AQ series)

### AQ1 — medium — a migration strands every burn made before kind 6 lands: a week at least, and the cure is one hand credit a burn

Plan: §5.3 "migrate (the lock, every claim, the sweep entries and the ids of undelivered sends go to a successor realm … from that page until the last the predecessor refuses every function and the successor credits nothing)"; §5.3 "kind 6's builder, for gno.land's own domain, accepts only a registered route's address".

Code: the successor is registered only when the migrate applies (`hlroute.gno:1583` `env.AddRoute` inside `apply`; `hlgov.gno:508–525` `isRouteKey` walks `hlmailbox.Routes()`), so kind 6 is proposed after that and lands 7 days after delivery. Until it lands the token's enrolled router is the predecessor, so every burn's message names the predecessor as recipient: the predecessor's `Process` panics (`hlroute.gno:494`, `mustNotBeMigrated` first in `Credit`), `hlmailbox.Process` refuses the successor (`mailbox.gno:453`, "only the message's recipient can take it"), the relayer's `routeFor` drops them (`relayer.go:449–456`). The `importing` gate (AP4) guards a window nothing can reach.

Scenario: Day 0 GovDAO executes hlgnot's migrate; day 7 it applies and the pages are pulled that afternoon; kind 6 is proposed and delivered the same day and lands day 14 (day 28 under a hold). Holders keep burning — burns are final, no flag stops them — 50 a day. Every burn of the window is addressed to the migrated predecessor: uncreditable by either realm, dropped by the relayer, "in flight" to the monitors for good. 350 burns, each a `credit by id` proposal of 7 days: two members vote 350 proposals, or the holders wait. The migration §1 calls the cure for a wedged route ends with a week of exits paid by hand.

Fix: hlmailbox records each route's successor at `AddRoute` and lets that successor take a message addressed to its predecessor, the successor's `Process` crediting it once importing ends (the message waits undelivered meanwhile); §5.3 states it, and the launch notice says burns made during a migration wait for the last page.

### AQ2 — medium — a refunded send stays deliverable on Solana, and the monitors read every refund as a break

Plan: §5.3 "refund a send by message id (7 days; the amount back from the lock to the sender, the send marked, so sentTotal and the lock fall together — the cure for a send the Solana side can never deliver, and a successor realm's duty by the same code)"; §5.4 "one refused for the per-message maximum (`OverMax`) stays parked until a kind 12 raises it".

Code: `apply(refundParams)` (`hlroute.gno:1559–1572`) marks the send and pays; nothing on Solana learns of it — the message stays in hlmailbox and the relayer's walk delivers it when it can (`relayer_sol.go:191–239`; an OverMax park is retried hourly). `readSends` (`monitor.go:735–780`) counts a send in `inflightOut` until Solana's processed record exists, and `Identity.Verdict` (`identity.go:68–88`) is BROKEN when right + in flight > lock — so from the refund on, by the refunded amount. No file under `agent/` or `cmd/` reads a refund.

Scenario: A 25,000 GNOT send strands OverMax (the route's maximum raised ahead of kind 12, §5.4's own hazard). GovDAO refunds it; the refund lands day 7. That minute both monitors read BROKEN — lock −25,000, the send still "in flight" — flag credits and payouts on hlgnot and the pausable ISM, and re-assert the Solana flag every minute until the operator rewrites the monitor: gno → Solana paused for both tokens. A month on, kind 12 raises the maximum; the relayer's hourly retry delivers the parked send and 25,000 is minted against a lock that refunded it. A successor's refunds after a migration do the same while the predecessor is still the enrolled router (AQ1's week).

Fix: the refund builder takes only imported sends, and hlwatch shows kind 6 has enrolled the successor first (the token then refuses them for good); a live route's stranded send waits for kind 12, never a refund (§5.3); the monitors and hlwatch read a send's `refunded` mark and drop it from in flight.

### AQ3 — low-medium — the watch judges a migrating token by one route's counters: BROKEN from page 0, the pausable ISM flagged every minute

Plan: §5.3 "the pause of both, which the watch reads from the successor's counters"; §5.9 "the identity `lock = sentTotal − paidTotal = Solana supply + owed + voided − lost` holds up to in-flight messages; a forged credit breaks it by its amount".

Code: `Export` page 0 moves every counter to the successor in one transaction (`hlroute.gno:1685–1688`; `Import` 1762–1768): the predecessor then shows lock 0 against the live supply, the successor the whole lock. The monitor judges each configured route alone (`monitor.go:389–398`) and reads neither `migratedTo` nor `importing` (only `wantClaim` does, `decisions.go:77`); BROKEN → `breakFlags` → `flagSolana` (`monitor.go:494–521`), re-asserted each minute.

Scenario: AQ1's day 7, the afternoon the pages are pulled. The operator's monitors still name the predecessor: at page 0 its right side is the supply over a lock of 0 — BROKEN: credits and payouts flagged on a route that refuses everything anyway, the pausable ISM flagged by the watch key and re-flagged every minute; both outsiders' hlwatch alarm BROKEN. Pointed at the successor before page 0, it reads the same. gno → Solana deliveries of both tokens stop until the operator clears its watch flag and stops the monitor, or GovDAO's kind 2 lands — in the middle of the operation the plan names as the cure for a wedged route.

Fix: the monitor and hlwatch read a route's `migratedTo`, `importing` and `imported` and judge one token over the pair's summed counters while a migration is open (the sum is invariant at every height), never flagging a migrated or importing route alone; §5.9 says so.

### AQ4 — low-medium — a kind 12 that lowers the limits below a parked send wedges the token's deliveries through the reservation

Plan: §5.7 "while a token's oldest such park waits, no younger message of that token that would leave it short is delivered, so a large send holds younger ones for at most its amount ÷ cap of a day"; §5.4 "one refused for the per-message maximum (`OverMax`) stays parked until a kind 12 raises it".

Code: a park is a cap park by its last error text (`relayer_sol.go:351–353`, `capParks` 365–378); `capGate` (384–402) reserves its amount against every younger message and lets the park itself go only when `bucketRoom` holds, which also demands `amount <= MaxPerMessage` (430–436). A park above the live maximum is never attempted, so its text never becomes OverMax and the reservation never lapses.

Scenario: An exit wave shrinks the GNOT lock. GovDAO lowers the route's maximum (at once) and then kind 12 to 5,000 a message and 10,000 a day, as §5.4's tenth of the lock asks. A 25,000 send parked OverDayCap the week before sits in the walk. From the day kind 12 lands the relayer holds every younger GNOT message behind it for good — the bucket can never hold 25,000 beside anything — the backup too; parked alarms repeat hourly and name the bucket, not the cause. The exits are a refund (AQ2) or a raise, 7 days each.

Fix: `capGate` reserves nothing for a park whose amount exceeds the live `MaxPerMessage` or `DayCap` and re-marks it OverMax (parked until a raise, reserving nothing); §5.7 says a lowering never holds younger sends.

### AQ5 — low — the announcement proves a realm exists, not that it is public or that it serves the route

Plan: §5.3 "the successor announces itself first (`AnnounceSuccessor`, its exact path), and the builder refuses an unannounced one, since a mistyped path would strand the lock or hand it to whoever may deploy there"; §5.3 "None of the five realms, nor any verifier realm, sets `private` … a re-upload would run `init()` again over the same package address, where the lock lives".

Code: `AnnounceSuccessor` records the caller's path alone (`flags.gno:320–336`); `DraftMigrate` checks the prefix, a `#`, self and the announcement (`hlroute.gno:1175–1184`); nothing reads the successor's gnomod.toml or the token it pays; §8.3's `private` check names the five realms; hlwatch's `programChecks` reads kinds 8 and 9 only (`main.go:246–262`).

Scenario: 2027: a community member deploys `r/<ns>/hlgnot2` with `private = true`, announces it from `init`, and asks GovDAO to migrate to it; the members read its source — honest — and the migrate lands with the lock. A month later the namespace owner re-uploads the path with code whose `init` skips the announcement and whose `Withdraw` pays him — the keeper lets a private package be replaced by a private one — and the lock is his. Or, by a slip, hlgnot's migrate names the BUBBLE successor, which announced itself just the same: the ugnot sits in a realm whose code never moves it.

Fix: §5.3 and §8.3 extend the `private` rule to a successor and the member's check reads its served gnomod.toml; `AnnounceSuccessor(cur, symbol)` records which route it succeeds and `DraftMigrate` refuses another's; hlwatch prints both beside a migrate proposal.

### AQ6 — low-medium — after a kind 9 every tool keeps watching, flagging and relaying to the predecessor until each operator and outsider edits a config; the successor holds every authority unwatched meanwhile

Plan: §6.5 "GovDAO proposes that switch only once `hlwatch` shows the kind 9 executed"; §5.8 "`hlwatch` … its flag command sets the holder's flag on the pausable ISM and on the admin program in one stroke (a hold must stand on the admin program's decisions to open recovery, §6.4)".

Code: every reading and every stroke keys on the config's programs — hlwatch's pending decisions, foreign list and seat check (`main.go:226`, `1008`, `1096` through `w.cfg.Programs()`), the monitor's `foreignDecisions` and `flagSolana` (`monitor_sol.go:81`, `363–367`), the relayer's `classify` (`relayer_sol.go:124–130`, a message to another recipient is "other"). hlwatch reads the predecessor's `successor` field only to print RECIPIENT SWITCH (`main.go:276–318`); nothing follows it.

Scenario: A leak (§1's stand-off): the validators' recovery carries a kind 9 to a clean successor; hold-opened, it executes on its 7th day. The thief, with the same keys, delivers forged decisions to the successor that hour — a kind 8 of the token program — maturing 7 days on. The outsiders' hlwatch still reads the predecessor: no FOREIGN line, and `hlwatch flag` puts their hold on the retired program; the monitors likewise. Unless each edits bridge.json with the successor's id within the week — a step no document names — the forged upgrade lands on the successor's 7th day with no hold on it. The relayer, too, delivers nothing GovDAO sends the successor until the operator reconfigures.

Fix: the tools read the admin program's `successor` from chain and follow it — decisions, foreign checks, pending, and the one-stroke flag on both states while the old still stands; §6.5 says the successor is watched from the block kind 9 executes, and the launch notice's bridge.json states the rule.

## Checked and held

- The sweep queue by id: a paid entry leaves `sweeps` and `credits` (hlroute.gno:1542–1547), so a void and a payment of one id in two proposals skip each other whichever lands first, a credit by id of a paid entry is "done already" (1627–1628), the sum is re-checked against `Sweep` and the lock at apply (1536–1541), 256 ids a proposal; a dust flood is cleared by payments to the treasurer, never voided as forged (that leaves the identity short by the dust).
- The oldest park under a griefer: four 25,000 sends a day take the bucket at 100,000 GNOT of capital a day (§9), the honest flow interleaving oldest-first behind each park for at most six hours; the reservation is the operator's relayer's courtesy, which a stranger's delivery ignores.
- The migration's pages: a replayed page is empty and `Import` refuses it by number (1757–1759); Pull and Import share one transaction, so a failed import exports nothing; a hand credit, restore or vote between pages lands above every imported place (base + `QueueNext`); the predecessor's counters are final once the migrate applied, every function refusing.
- The announcement: keyed by the caller's own exact path, a sub-identity refused (flags.gno:328), so nobody announces for another's path; one made long before binds nothing until a migrate names it (AQ5 is what it does not prove).
- The recipient switch: `Redispatch` by anyone resends the same number, which a successor at the predecessor's high-water mark ignores when recorded (processor.rs:350–353, 884); a decision pending on the predecessor at kind 9 is dead and re-proposed, as §6.5 says; a hold set on the successor before the switch defers nothing pending; the successor's copied hold history holds only ended holds (`guardians` fresh, 168), so no floor carries over.
- `hlrecover execute`: the program checks every passed account against the record; `extendPlan` refuses a growth above 0.5 SOL without `-yes`; the relayer's `extendOnce` pays from its float after alarming (§6.5's own words), and a padded 790 KB buffer exceeds the float and parks.
- `mint:cap-ratio`: compiled as the smaller of 100,000 and a tenth of each token's expected lock, one value for both (setup.go:70–78); it alarms from the first poll until a token's lock reaches half its expectation, the launch weeks' own picture, kind 12 (7 days) the only mover — an alarm the members should expect to stand.
- The node's minimum fee: paid once per call under max(10× the block-price fee, 1 GNOT) or `gno.maxNodeFee` (gno.go:371–380); `Claim` backs off while nothing is paid, so a hostile minimum on the operator's own node drains at the call rate and trips the 7-day runway alarm, never silently.
- The retired predecessor: `close_cancelled` is permissionless once `successor` is set (processor.rs:586–603), the rent to the recorded deliverer (546) and a staged buffer to its recorded refund key (567–574), nothing divertible; `stage_upgrade` and `record` answer Retired (520, recovery.rs:301); `handle` answers Retired before the number is read (336–342), so the message takes no number and stays deliverable.
- MAX_PARAMS 10,135 by one account's growth (recovery.rs:196); a mailbox body is bounded far lower by r/hlgov's `mustFit` at creation and at dispatch (hlgov.gno:213–223, 269, 348), a kind 5 of four entries 142 bytes, a cancel of sixteen 163; the self-transfer is spent before any extra account (processor.rs:689–696) and refused by r/hlgov at creation and in the closure (570–574) and by hlwatch (362).
- The ring epoch: one up at every `Ring::clear` — a set change (client processor.rs:585) and a reset (759–761) — and the stage's copy is read only under the same epoch and set hash (677–683), so a stale copy after a reset or across a set change is NotInRing; the pausable ISM reads the 64-byte head alone (`read_checked`, VERSION_1, pausable processor.rs:298) and the admin program the body through the client crate, both built with it.
- The unindexed-credit flag: it runs only after the cut holds and both scans succeeded with nothing skipped, after one more poll (monitor.go:838–866); a forged credit with no dispatch breaks the exact rule in the same poll anyway, so the rule is a belt, and a provider pair whose index lags twenty seconds costs an operator chore (the watch key's own clear), never a loss. The economics: 9.0 of rent + 0.15 + 1.5 = 10.65 against 7.36, the 8 SOL summing to the lamport (3.2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0.2 + 0.1 + 0.3 + 0.1 + 1.1); a successor admin program's 2.03 stays unbudgeted, as round 3 said.

## Verdict

Honest for a GovDAO member on every ending §1 promises, with two qualifications about the cure §1 leans on most, `migrate`: as built, a migration strands every burn made in the week or more before kind 6 enrolls the successor, each a hand credit (AQ1), and the watch reads the move itself as a forgery and pauses deliveries (AQ3); and a refund — the plan's cure for a stranded send — voids nothing on Solana, so the send can still mint after a kind 12 raise, while the monitors read every refund as a break (AQ2). The rest are small guards: a lowering kind 12 wedging a token behind a parked send (AQ4), the announced successor unchecked for `private` and for its route (AQ5), and the tools following the config, not the chain, across a kind 9 (AQ6). All buildable without inventing a rule. Nothing above medium; AP1–AP7 hold.
